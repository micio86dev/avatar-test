# BARS Authoring Standard (Italian) — House Voice and the Anti-Hedge Rule

**Status**: binding, written once, and a stated PREREQUISITE — no Italian BARS,
competency, or role content may be merged before this document is committed
and reviewed (see `framework-catalog-it-translations` proposal, Decision 3;
spec delta `framework-catalog`, Requirement: Italian Authoring Standard
Precedes Content).

**Sibling, not a subset.** This document is the Italian-language counterpart
to
`docs/app_description/02-domain/framework-authoring/house-voice-and-anti-hedge-standard.md`
(the English standard, "the EN standard" below). It does not replace or
narrow the EN standard's authority over the ENGLISH catalogue — it governs
Italian *translation* of that catalogue, produced under
`framework-catalog-it-translations`. Roughly half of the EN standard is
language-specific (bare-infinitive spelling conventions, `-ize` spellings,
ASCII-apostrophe-only, English deficit-verb inventory) and does not survive
translation unchanged; this document restates each such rule in its Italian
form rather than assuming the EN document applies by analogy.

**This is a translation standard, not a re-authoring standard.** The Italian
text must say what the English says (see the change proposal's Constraint 1).
Where §1–§3 below describe Italian GRAMMATICAL FORM, they constrain HOW an
English sentence is rendered in Italian — never license improving, softening,
or sharpening content the English does not already contain.

## 1. House voice, in Italian

### 1.1 Indicator — infinitive form

The Italian indicator form is the **bare infinitive**, mirroring the EN
leader-file form (bare infinitive, no subject, no terminal period):

- **Bare infinitive**, no subject pronoun (`Individuare i sintomi che
  indicano un problema`, not `Individua i sintomi...` and never `Il candidato
  individua...`).
- **No terminal period.**
- **No compound of two unrelated acts** — exactly as the EN standard's
  "one observable act" rule; a translation must not merge or split the
  English indicator's single behaviour.
- **Never names the competency** in Italian any more than the English does.
- Uses **`proprio/propria/propri/proprie`** (agreeing in gender/number with
  the noun) as the possessive where English uses `own` (`own team` → `propria
  squadra`; `own area` → `propria area`; `own positions` → `proprie
  posizioni`) — never a bare possessive pronoun (`suo/sua`) where `proprio`
  reads more naturally and preserves the EN's deliberate avoidance of
  gendered/possessive ambiguity.

**Worked example** (mapping, not authored content): EN `Recognize symptoms
that indicate problems` → IT `Individuare i sintomi che indicano un
problema`.

### 1.2 Anchor — subject-elided third person

The Italian anchor form is **subject-elided third-person present** — Italian
freely drops the subject pronoun with a conjugated verb, which is the
Italian equivalent of the EN standard's own subject-elision rule (EN cannot
elide its subject grammatically, so it uses bare third-person `-s` conjugation
instead: `Recognizes...`). The Italian form is the conjugated verb alone,
with NO subject pronoun (`lui`/`lei`/`esso`) ever inserted:

- **`Individua...`**, not `Lui individua...` or `Il candidato individua...`.
- Describes what a person at that level DOES (level 5, level 3) or FAILS TO
  DO (level 1) — never an instruction, never addressed to the reader (no
  imperative mood, no `tu`/`Lei` form).
- Mirrors the EN anchor's clause shape per level:

| Level | EN shape | IT shape |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Strong verb/frequency adverb opens; often an impact clause closes it | Verbo forte o avverbio di frequenza in apertura (`Costantemente`, `Proattivamente`, `Promuove`, `Guida`); spesso una proposizione di impatto in chiusura (`con risultati misurabili`, `guadagnando fiducia duratura`) |
| 3 | Opens with a verb naming what is done | Apre con un verbo che nomina l'azione compiuta |
| 1 | Deficit verb opens; often a consequence clause closes it | Verbo di carenza in apertura (§1.4 below); spesso una proposizione di conseguenza in chiusura (`generando confusione o disallineamento`, `minando la fiducia del team`) |

**An anchor names a behaviour, not a frequency of the indicator** — same
rule as the EN standard, unchanged by translation: the indicator is the act,
the anchor is *how* that act is performed and *on what object*.

### 1.3 Register — professional/neutral, never regional

**Professional/neutral business Italian.** This is product content read by
candidates and clients across Italy (and by Italian-speaking clients
elsewhere); it MUST NOT carry regional lexicon, regional syntax, or
colloquial register of any kind. Concretely:

- Standard Italian ("italiano standard"/"italiano neutro"), the register used
  in national business publications and formal HR documentation — not a
  regional variety (no Tuscan, Lombard, Southern, or any other regionalism in
  vocabulary or construction), and not colloquial/spoken-register Italian
  (no `dai`, `boh`, clitic doubling, or other informal-register markers).
- **No second person.** Never `tu` or `Lei` forms, never addresses the
  reader — mirrors the EN standard's "no second person" rule exactly.
- **No `il candidato` / `il dipendente` as subject** — anchors describe a
  behaviour in the abstract third person (subject-elided, §1.2), not a named
  evaluated person. Same rule as EN, same reason.
- **No modal obligation** (`dovrebbe`, `deve`, `è tenuto a`) — anchors
  describe what someone *does*, not what they are instructed to do.
- **No numeric targets** (`aumenta le vendite del 20%`) — same as EN.
- **Business vocabulary no heavier than the EN standard's own ceiling**
  (`SWOT` is the EN reference point; the Italian equivalent register is the
  vocabulary of a standard Italian business-management text, not a
  specialist or academic register).

### 1.4 Deficit-verb inventory (level 1)

The EN inventory (`Fails to`, `Struggles to`, `Rarely`, `Avoids`, `Neglects`,
`Resists`, `Does not`) carries level 1's force through DEFICIT — an absence
or failure of the level-3/level-5 behaviour — not through added severity or
judgment the English does not itself express. The Italian inventory below is
selected on the same basis: each entry names an absence or failure, not a
character judgment, and none of them is stronger in register than its EN
counterpart.

| EN | IT (deficit form) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fails to | Non riesce a / Non arriva a | `Non riesce a` for a competence gap; `Non arriva a` when the EN implies falling short of a bar |
| Struggles to | Fatica a | Direct cognate register; no added severity |
| Rarely | Raramente | Frequency adverb, same as EN |
| Avoids | Evita di | |
| Neglects | Trascura di | |
| Resists | Resiste a / Si oppone a | `Resiste a` for passive resistance, `Si oppone a` only where the EN anchor itself names active opposition, never as a default upgrade |
| Does not | Non | Bare negation, same register as EN's bare `Does not` |

**Binding rule**: a translator MUST select the IT deficit verb whose degree
matches the EN source verb's degree, from this table or a table addendum
following the same method — never a verb that is more severe in Italian than
the EN original is in English, and never one that is milder (Constraint 1:
translation fidelity, not re-authoring, in either direction).

### 1.5 Orthography

- **Accents**: grave accent on stressed final vowels (`città`, `perché`,
  `così`) per standard Italian orthography — never the acute accent
  substituted for the grave (`cosí` is wrong), and never an apostrophe used
  in place of an accent (`perche'` is wrong; `perché` is correct).
- **Apostrophes**: standard Italian elision apostrophe (`l'obiettivo`,
  `un'analisi`) using the ASCII straight apostrophe (`'`, U+0027) — the same
  ASCII-only convention the EN standard applies to English contractions,
  applied here to Italian elision. Do not use the curly/typographic
  apostrophe (U+2019, `'`).
- **Do not copy legacy defects** found in the EN catalogue (see the EN
  standard §1.3's own note on legacy typos) into the Italian translation —
  a faithful translation reproduces the EN *meaning*, not an EN *spelling
  error*. This does not extend to the EN standard's own §2.1 hedge-only
  differentiation defect (see §2 below), which the translation IS required
  to carry forward faithfully.

## 2. Constraint 1, applied to Italian specifically

**Translation fidelity, not re-authoring.** The change proposal
(`framework-catalog-it-translations`) states this as a binding constraint:
where the English is weak — including the anti-hedge defect the EN standard's
own §2 documents (76% of legacy level-3 anchors are hedge-only; ICO alone
89%) — the Italian translation MUST NOT silently improve it. A translator who
notices a level-5/level-3 pair in English that differs only by a degree
adverb (the EN standard's own "done wrong" example, §2.1) must translate that
same hedge-only pair faithfully into Italian, producing the same weakness in
Italian, not a corrected one.

This is not a contradiction of quality: it is what makes cross-locale scoring
comparable. Two languages that score differently on the same rubric is a
worse outcome than both scoring equally weakly on a known, recorded defect —
see the proposal's Risk 1 and Risk 2, and design decision D2a (the
`catalog_crosslocale_duplicate_divergence` guard), which is the one
mechanical check that can catch a translator improving (or flattening) the
English during translation.

If a specific pair is judged too weak to ship even in translation, that is a
retro-fix proposal that repairs BOTH languages in the same commit — never an
Italian-only correction (proposal Out of Scope: "Retro-fixing the English
hedge defect").

## 3. Cross-role differentiation, in Italian

The EN standard's cross-role differentiation rule (§3) is a property of the
underlying ENGLISH content — role-specific object/horizon/accountability
differences already exist in the English being translated — and a faithful
Italian translation of two already-different English anchors will itself
differ, by construction, unless the translation flattens a real EN
distinction into the same Italian wording. That flattening is exactly what
`catalog_crosslocale_duplicate_divergence` is built to catch (design D2a):
Italian converging where English differs is as much a Constraint-1 violation
as Italian diverging where English is byte-identical.

The one legitimate EXCEPTION is a pair the EN catalogue itself already
records as a cross-role duplicate (`framework-crossrole-baseline.txt`,
locale-qualified per design D2a) — two roles whose ENGLISH text is
byte-identical for that competency. A faithful translation of two identical
English sentences is two identical Italian sentences; rewording the Italian
to manufacture a difference the English does not have is itself a
Constraint-1 violation (differentiating Italian where English does not),
not a fix.

**Do not fill this table before the pilot.** The translator authoring the 36
pilot anchors (`PRS` × {FLL, MLL, BUL, SRX}) MUST NOT be told any word-count

## 5. Using this document

1. Read the EN standard in full, and the specific English pairs being
   translated, before authoring any Italian.
2. Read `docs/app_description/02-domain/framework-authoring/it/bilingual/`
   for the generated EN|IT review table of the pairs already translated —
   generated by `scripts/framework-bilingual-review.js`, never hand-edited.
3. Translate following §1 (voice/form) and §1.4 (deficit-verb selection),
   preserving the EN's own level-differentiation exactly (§2) — including
   where it is weak.
4. Run the mechanical checks locally: `catalog_malformed_bars_entries`,
   `catalog_overlong_bars_anchors` (against the Italian ceiling once §4 is
   filled), `catalog_crossrole_duplicates`, and
   `catalog_crosslocale_duplicate_divergence` (all in `scripts/ci-guards.sh`).
5. This document requires native-Italian-speaker + assessment-domain review
   before any content PR built against it is merged (Decision 3; no code gate
   enforces this — it is a stated prerequisite, checked at review, exactly
   like the EN standard's own hedge-rate ceiling in EN §2.3).
